# Design spec — Sheng puer educational program

**Date:** 2026-08-30
**Type:** Internal research guide for a future tea post
**Status:** Approved design → ready for research plan

## 1. Purpose

Create a practical, modular guide for learning how variables within **sheng puer** affect the cup. The guide will organize suggested teas into comparative flights and tell the reader:

- what to compare;
- why each tea is educationally useful or significant;
- what sensory differences deserve attention;
- where the tea is currently available;
- what it costs in euros; and
- what to substitute when the named tea is unavailable.

The teas are suggestions, not a mandatory shopping list. The guide must work for beginners, experienced drinkers, and different budgets. It is an internal foundation for a later post, not the post itself.

## 2. Scope decisions

- **Tea only:** cover origin, material, season, processing, recipe, producer, age, and storage. Exclude water, teaware, and brewing experiments.
- **Worldwide sourcing:** consider vendors in Asia, Europe, and North America.
- **Pricing:** EUR; exclude shipping, tax, and import costs.
- **Format:** 12 core flights plus 12 optional advanced flights.
- **Flights:** normally two teas; use three only where a triangle or vertical materially improves the lesson.
- **No tasting procedure:** provide a focused “pay attention to” section, but no instructions for conducting or recording a session.
- **Honesty:** distinguish documented facts, vendor claims, and practitioner expectations. State confounding variables instead of presenting comparisons as controlled experiments.

## 3. Curriculum model

Use a **spiral curriculum**. Begin with large, legible contrasts, then revisit the same variables through tighter comparisons. Reuse approximately 14–18 anchor teas across the 12 core flights instead of requiring 24 unrelated purchases. Show both the incremental cost of a flight and the cumulative cost of a path.

### Core flights

| # | Flight | Target comparison | Pay attention to | Principal limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Youth and transformation | Recent Menghai 7542 vs a naturally stored 2005–2011 7542 | Color, fresh vs tertiary aroma, bitterness onset, astringency, integration, returning sweetness, finish | Recipe name does not hold material and manufacture constant across eras |
| 2 | Storage changes the clock | Representative cool/dry-stored vs warm/humid-stored factory sheng | Retained youth, color, sharpness, softened structure, storage aroma, thickness | This is an intentionally broad orientation flight, not a matched experiment |
| 3 | Factory and boutique | Recent large-factory blend vs recent boutique production of similar age and price | Compression, smoke, aromatic clarity, structure, immediate accessibility, durability | Factory/boutique combines material, processing, blending, and intended use |
| 4 | Yiwu and Bulang | Same producer/year, matched processing and tier where possible | Aromatic lift, sweetness, softness, bitterness, huigan, coating texture, finish | Both regions are internally diverse; tendencies are not terroir laws |
| 5 | One tea, two storages | Same production and batch after years in two climates | Transformation, top-note retention, storage aroma, integrated structure, body, spent leaf | City names do not capture warehouse conditions, airflow, or wrapping |
| 6 | 7542 and 8582 | Same period, factory, and storage environment | Leaf size, aromatic register, bitterness, breadth, thickness, infusion curve, durability | Recipe implementation varies by year and batch |
| 7 | Menghai and Xiaguan | 7542 vs 8653 or equivalent, matched by period and storage | Smoke, compression effects, sharpness, bitterness, mouthfeel, sweetness, factory signature | Factory, recipe, material, and compression all differ |
| 8 | Xishuangbanna and Lincang | Same producer/year and market tier | Fragrance type, sweetness, bitterness/astringency balance, cooling, texture, persistence | Both prefectures are large; producer style may dominate |
| 9 | Boutique lineage through time | Young vs 10–20-year-old tea from one producer, region, or lineage | Origin character, aromatic evolution, integration, texture, depth, finish | Sourcing and processing may change between vintages |
| 10 | Natural humid and traditional HK storage | Similar-period naturally aged vs traditionally stored Hong Kong sheng | Cellar/camphor notes, earthiness, clarity, transformation depth, body, cleanliness | Storage terminology is inconsistent and histories may be incomplete |
| 11 | Blend and single origin | Same producer/year/tier where possible | Aromatic focus, transitions, balance, structure, regional character, durability | Origin claims are difficult to verify; processing may dominate |
| 12 | Three-age vertical | Young, mid-aged, and older examples of one recipe or lineage | What disappears, persists, and emerges; bitterness integration; texture; non-linear change | Batch, era, storage, and market selection remain uncontrolled |

Flight 12 should reuse teas from earlier flights wherever possible.

### Optional advanced flights

**Terroir within terroir**

13. Yiwu village or subregion comparison from one producer.
14. Bitter Lao Man’e-style material vs a sweeter or more balanced Bulang example.
15. Mengku-side vs Bangdong/Xigui-side Lincang material.

**Material and processing**

16. Spring vs autumn material from the same origin and producer.
17. Traditional aging-oriented vs modern fragrance-forward processing.
18. Plantation vs teas sold as old-arbor material, with explicit provenance caveats.

**Recipes, factories, and brands**

19. Dayi recipe suite: 7532, 7542, and 8582 from a matched era.
20. Xiaguan recipes and forms: 8653 iron cake vs a tuo or another major recipe.
21. Boutique philosophies: Taiwanese boutique, Western-facing blender, and origin-focused producer.

**Storage and market literacy**

22. Same production from Kunming, Taiwan/Guangdong, and Malaysia where sourcing permits.
23. Traditional Hong Kong storage at different intensities or stages of airing.
24. Famous-area tea vs credible neighboring material at a fraction of the price.

Advanced flights are an elective menu, not a second required sequence. Label each by difficulty and expected cost.

## 4. Flight entry template

Each finished flight should contain only the information needed to choose and understand the comparison:

1. **Comparison and lesson** — the variable and learning question.
2. **Why it belongs** — why this distinction matters in sheng puer.
3. **Suggested teas** — full name, year, producer, recipe/origin, storage, and current vendor.
4. **Why these teas** — significance, special qualities, and representativeness of each selection.
5. **Pay attention to** — only the sensory signals relevant to this flight.
6. **What it cannot prove** — the important confounders and provenance limits.
7. **Alternatives** — lower-cost, equivalent, and advanced suggestions plus a durable substitution rule.
8. **Budget** — minimum purchase cost, normalized €/10 g, and any reused anchor tea.

Exact teas are recommendations, not rules. Every flight must remain usable after a product sells out.

## 5. Budget model

Provide three paths where the market permits:

- **Essential:** smallest credible samples, maximum anchor reuse.
- **Standard:** more representative teas and tighter comparative matching.
- **Advanced:** older, rarer, or historically important examples.

For each listing record the original price/currency, purchasable quantity, converted EUR price, €/10 g, vendor country, and date checked. Show:

- incremental cost per flight;
- cumulative core-path cost;
- advanced-module cost; and
- order-consolidation opportunities.

Do not imply that expensive tea is inherently better. Explain what a higher-priced example adds educationally. Flag cases where multiple international shipments undermine an otherwise inexpensive basket.

## 6. Research and sourcing

Use [TeaDB’s puer section](https://teadb.org/puerh/) as the curriculum map and [The Five Types of Raw Pu’erh You Should Try](https://teadb.org/five-types-raw-puerh/) as the initial category model. Use TeaDB’s regional primers, storage material, factory coverage, focused drinking reports, buying analysis, and [2025 non-mainland vendor guide](https://teadb.org/non-mainland-puerh-vendor-guide/) to identify candidate comparisons.

TeaDB is the **compass**, not the inventory source. Verify current stock, sizes, storage descriptions, and prices on vendors’ own pages.

Initial vendor pool:

- **Semi-aged/aged:** Teas We Like, Quiche Teas/Taishunhe, The Jade Leaf, Liquid Proust, Bana Tea, The Steeping Room, Hou De, Teapals, Puerh.uk, Yangqing Hao USA, and Xizi Hao.
- **Young/boutique:** Yunnan Sourcing, White2Tea, Farmer Leaf, Essence of Tea, Crimson Lotus, Bitterleaf, Tea Encounter, Pu-erh.sk, and Tea Urchin.
- **Traditional Hong Kong storage:** especially Yee On Tea.
- **Mainland/dry-stored factory:** Yunnan Sourcing, Chawangshop, and King Tea Mall.

Selection rules:

- Recommend individual teas, not vendors wholesale.
- Prefer samples; cake-only recommendations normally belong in the advanced tier.
- Prefer documented batch and storage history over broad labels.
- For same-production comparisons, check year, batch, wrapper, weight, and recipe.
- Mark educational but unavailable teas separately from purchasable recommendations.
- Treat famous-village, tree-age, and boutique-provenance claims with heightened caution.
- Use recent TeaDB reports to update older hub material.

## 7. Final internal guide structure

Keep the internal document concise:

1. brief usage map;
2. reusable core shopping basket;
3. 12 core flight entries;
4. 12 advanced flight entries;
5. vendor/availability index by specialty;
6. essential, standard, and advanced budget summaries;
7. minimal beginner glossary; and
8. sources and confidence notes.

The later public post may add narrative, voice, and broader explanation. Those are not part of this program document.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All 12 core and 12 advanced flights have a clear comparison and learning value.
- [ ] Every core flight includes specific currently purchasable suggestions and at least one substitution rule.
- [ ] Every suggested tea explains why it is useful and what is special about it.
- [ ] Every flight has a short, specific “pay attention to” section and an honest confounder note.
- [ ] Core recommendations support beginner, standard, and advanced budgets where supply allows.
- [ ] Prices are dated, converted correctly to EUR, and shown as both minimum spend and €/10 g.
- [ ] Cumulative totals do not double-count reused anchor teas.
- [ ] Current product links resolve at the research date; unavailable exemplars are clearly labeled.
- [ ] TeaDB guides candidate discovery; vendor pages support current purchasing facts.
- [ ] Factual provenance is separated from vendor claims and practitioner interpretation.
- [ ] No brewing, water, teaware, or tasting-session instructions appear.
- [ ] The result remains concise enough to function as an internal post-definition document.

## 9. Out of scope

- Drafting or publishing the blog post.
- Brewing parameters, water comparisons, teaware, or session worksheets.
- Ripe puer.
- Home aging or storage instructions.
- Comprehensive vendor rankings or endorsements.
- Treating sensory tendencies as scientifically controlled terroir rules.
