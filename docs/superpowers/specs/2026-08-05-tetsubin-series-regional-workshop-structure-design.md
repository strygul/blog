# Tetsubin Series Regional and Workshop Structure

## Goal

Make Parts 1–3 easier to read and give Parts 2 and 3 the same flexible workshop-profile structure. Part 1 will explain the regional geography once, while all three posts reuse one map.

## Shared map

- Add one shared Japan map and reuse the same file in Parts 1–3.
- Use an existing blank outline of Japan as the base.
- Mark only five regional centers: Morioka, Mizusawa (present-day Ōshū), Yamagata, Kyoto–Ōmi, and Takaoka.
- Explain that Nambu ironware is a modern craft name covering Morioka and Mizusawa/Ōshū, not another location.
- Keep labels large and readable on narrow screens. Do not require horizontal scrolling.

## Part 1

- Keep the post focused on the birth and wider history of the tetsubin.
- Rewrite `One Country, Several Casting Histories` in simple language.
- Place the shared map in that section.
- Give each regional center two or three clear sentences explaining its role in the series.
- Remove the Morioka display photo from the regional overview because Part 2 covers Morioka in detail.
- Remove licensing and image-production commentary from the published text while retaining useful source links.

## Part 2: Morioka

- Keep the title format `Tetsubin History, Part 2: Morioka and Nambu Ironware`.
- Open with a short introduction to Morioka and reuse the shared map.
- Keep a short regional history and a concise explanation of the common casting process.
- Profile Suzuki Morihisa, Kunzan, Iwachu, and Kamasada.
- Each profile should follow this order where the evidence allows:
  1. Introduce the workshop and its location.
  2. Give a short history.
  3. Explain what makes its work different.
  4. Show representative work.
  5. Explain useful pattern or construction clues without presenting them as proof.
  6. Show and explain the workshop seal or mark.
- Keep each workshop as one substantial `##` section rather than splitting it into many small blocks.
- Reuse the existing work and seal images.
- Remove licensing, reuse, cropping, and recompression language from captions.

## Part 3: Mizusawa and Ōshū

- Keep the title format `Tetsubin History, Part 3: Mizusawa and Ōshū`.
- Open with a short explanation of Mizusawa's place inside present-day Ōshū and reuse the shared map.
- Keep only the minimum comparison with Morioka needed to explain the Nambu name, with a link to Part 2.
- Remove the Morioka kettle picture and repeated Morioka workshop history.
- Give a short regional history centered on Mizusawa's practical casting tradition.
- Profile Oigen and Oitomi using the same flexible order as the Part 2 workshop sections.
- Reuse current representative work images and add missing work or seal images only when a reliable workshop or product source exists.
- If a workshop does not publish a dependable kettle-seal guide, say that plainly instead of inventing an identification rule.
- Remove licensing and image-production commentary from the published text and captions.

## Language and layout

- Prefer short sentences and common words.
- Keep paragraphs focused on one idea.
- Avoid repeated caveats, research-process language, dense tables, and small diagrams.
- Use `##` headings for substantial sections.
- Use the existing centered figure and studio-gallery styles so images remain fully visible at desktop and narrow widths.
- Keep source links close to factual claims and image captions.
