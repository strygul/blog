## Evidence labels

- **Documented:** supported by a manufacturer record, identifiable production, or specific storage provenance.
- **Vendor claim:** stated by the seller but not independently verified.
- **Practitioner expectation:** recurring sensory or historical interpretation from TeaDB or another specialist source.

## Age and storage synthesis

**Sources checked:** 2026-08-30. TeaDB's category language guides the lesson; the thresholds below are a practical program convention rather than a scientific maturity scale.

- **Young:** under 10 years for this program. TeaDB does not supply an explicit lower-band cutoff, but contrasts young tea with its defined 10–20-year semi-aged group. A light/yellow liquor and fresher or more floral profile are **practitioner expectations**, not guaranteed facts.
- **Semi-aged:** 10–20 years, matching TeaDB's Five Types wording. TeaDB expects age and warmer storage generally to darken and smooth a tea, but production, compression, and storage history remain confounders.
- **Older:** over 20 years for this program. This is an operational bucket, not a TeaDB definition; age alone does not document maturation quality, authenticity, or storage.
- **Natural warm/humid storage:** ambient or ordinary warehouse/home storage in places such as Guangdong, Taiwan, Malaysia, or Xishuangbanna. TeaDB expects faster transformation and darker orange-to-red liquor than in cool/dry storage. A city name does not establish actual temperature, humidity, airflow, cleanliness, or continuity.
- **Cool/dry storage:** naturally dry storage (for example Kunming) or deliberately dry storage within a humid region. TeaDB describes slower transformation and greater retention of the original profile. Its rough 60–70% RH and 60–70 °F range is descriptive, not a storage prescription.
- **Traditional Hong Kong storage:** an intentionally intense commercial process: densely packed tea first spends time in a warm, humid ground warehouse, then moves to a drier location to rest and air. TeaDB distinguishes this skilled process from ordinary humid storage and from the vague label “wet stored.” Exact cellar time, rotation, and post-cellar airing must come from the offer; otherwise the comparison is orientation only.
- **Compression warning:** TeaDB notes that iron cakes and other tight compressions age more slowly under otherwise similar conditions. No flight should attribute a difference solely to climate when compression differs.

### TeaDB benchmark recipes and boutique lineages

- The mid-2000s factory baseline names 2006/2008 Dayi 8582, 2006/2007/2008/2010 Dayi 7542, and early-year 2005 Xiaguan T8653 Thick Paper. These are benchmark search recipes, not authentication evidence.
- The Five Types guide's ideal semi-aged warm/humid example is a 2005–2011 Menghai Tea Factory tea stored in Taiwan; its explicit boutique example is 2007 Chenyuan Hao Yiwu.
- The Yiwu report supplies longer boutique lineages for study: Chenyuan Hao, Yangqing Hao, Biyun Hao, Xizi Hao, and Wistaria/Baohongyinji. TeaDB particularly identifies early 2004–2005 Biyun Hao, the 2003/2005 Wistaria Zipin comparison, 2004–2007 Yangqing Hao, and Chenyuan Hao productions available with both Malaysian and Taiwanese storage.
- TeaDB's ratings, flavor notes, body-feel reports, storage preferences, and predictions about future aging are **practitioner observations**. They are useful for setting tasting hypotheses only; they do not document the identity, condition, or expected sensory result of another vendor's cake.

### Flights 9–12: interacting variables and synthesis

- Reuse the 2025/2015/2007 Dayi 7542 set for a three-year recipe vertical spanning young through late-semi-aged tea. Year, batch, and stated storage differ, so conclusions must remain multivariable. Pair the 2000 Yee On 7542 only when an over-20-years operational example is needed; producer and recipe align, but vendor and storage history do not.
- **Boutique lineage comparison:** Liquid Proust currently offers 16 g each of 2024 Xizi Hao Yun Tai 8582 and 2007 Xizi Hao Diangu. Different recipes/origins and undocumented storage make it a lineage comparison, not a maturation control.
- **Traditional-storage core choices:** Yee On's 10 g 2000 7542 and 25 g 2008 “Taste of Hong Kong” both explicitly name traditional Hong Kong warehouse treatment; the latter specifies 12 years in a basement warehouse. They differ in producer, recipe, age, and treatment duration, so they are two viable orientations rather than a controlled pair. The 10 g 2000 7532 is a same-year/factory substitute for the 7542 whose page supplies less process detail. Bana's 50 g 2000 Menghai-area Hong Kong-warehouse tea is an orientation alternate only because its page does not document traditional cellar treatment.
- **Substitution rule:** preserve raw tea, the intended recipe where possible, and the same practical age band; require explicit traditional-Hong-Kong wording plus a stated post-cellar airing/rest history for a controlled replacement. If airing history is absent, label the replacement “traditional-storage orientation,” never an exact match.

## Rejected candidates

- Do not enter a generic recipe label (for example, an unlabeled “7542”) without evidence that it is the stated factory production and batch.
- Do not use a city name as a substitute for storage provenance.
- Do not use cheap famous-village or highly specific old-arbor claims as terroir proof; retain them only as explicitly caveated market-literacy examples if a future flight needs one.
- Use cake-only offers only in advanced recommendations, except where a cake is unusually affordable; label the exception and limitation explicitly.
- Do not turn TeaDB's historical price references, ratings, or vendor mentions into current prices, stock status, or documented provenance.
- Unavailable catalog candidates: Teas We Like 2005 Dayi 7542 batch 504 Taiwan/Malaysia. The two exact variants remain structured rows for Task 5/11 rechecking, not purchasable recommendations.

## Research caveats

- TeaDB provides practitioner interpretation and discovery; vendor product pages provide time-sensitive stock, size, currency, price, and stated storage facts.
- A vendor's stated origin, village, tree age, storage, or batch is a **vendor claim** unless the offer supplies stronger documentation. Sensory descriptions are **practitioner expectations**, not controlled results.
- Production year, recipe, producer, leaf material, compression, processing, storage environment, and sampling history can all confound a comparison.
- TeaDB sources span 2014–2026. Their vendor and price commentary can age quickly, so the catalog must carry its own checked date and no unverified purchasing fact is implied here.
