**Research started:** 2026-08-30
**Purpose:** Evidence, candidate selection, price provenance, and rejected options for the internal educational program.

## Evidence labels

- **Documented:** supported by a manufacturer record, identifiable production, or specific storage provenance.
- **Vendor claim:** stated by the seller but not independently verified.
- **Practitioner expectation:** recurring sensory or historical interpretation from TeaDB or another specialist source.

## Candidate and pairing notes

These are research directions, not recommendations or claims of availability. Candidate names are retained here to guide later vendor-page verification; only verified offers belong in the catalog.

### Flights 1–2: age and broad storage

- Start with a recent Dayi 7542 and a 2005–2011 7542 with named natural storage; TeaDB's five-types guide gives Taiwan-stored Menghai as the pedagogical target.
- Use the same production only when year, batch, wrapper, cake weight, and two distinct storage histories can be documented. Otherwise label Flight 2 an orientation comparison.

### Flights 3, 6–7: production models, recipes, and factories

- Mid-2000s factory anchors to search first: 2006/2008 Dayi 8582, 2006–2010 Dayi 7542, and 2005 Xiaguan T8653 thick paper.
- TeaDB's recipes reports make 2005 7542 502, 2005 8582 504, 2006 7542/8582/7742, and 2008 8582 801 useful discovery terms, not automatic selections.

### Flights 4 and 8: introductory terroir

- Use Yiwu versus Bulang and Xishuangbanna versus Lincang as broad, legible regional lessons. Prefer same producer/year/market tier when a defensible match exists.
- Treat village names and old-arbor assertions as vendor claims, especially Bingdao, Xigui, Chawangshu, Bohetang, and Mansong.

### Flight 5: matched storage

- A true anchor requires the same production and batch with separately documented storage. TeaDB's storage writing makes clear that city names alone cannot establish comparable warehouse conditions.

### Flights 9–12: interacting variables and synthesis

- Search one producer or lineage through time rather than assuming a recipe alone fixes material or manufacture. Reuse prior Dayi/factory anchors for the three-age vertical.
- Use naturally humid versus traditional Hong Kong storage only with explicit provenance; traditional storage, dry storage, and ordinary humid home storage are different categories.

### Flights 13–15: advanced terroir

- Mine the Yiwu report for same-producer Yiwu village/subregion candidates and the Bulang report for bitter, sweet-converting, and blended Menghai-area candidates.
- For Lincang, search Mengku-side and Bangdong/Xigui-side material while preserving the same claim caveats and avoiding a price-led microregion purchase.

### Flights 16–18: material and processing

- These flights need unusually strong production records: same producer/origin across spring and autumn, and explicit processing or material claims.
- Do not present plantation/old-arbor labels as verified facts without credible provenance; the educational value can instead be the uncertainty itself.

### Flights 19–21: advanced recipes and brands

- A matched-era Dayi 7532/7542/8582 suite is a targeted search, with 2006 and 2008 as practical starting eras from the reports.
- Xiaguan T8653/iron-cake material and Taiwanese boutique, Western-facing blender, and origin-focused producer examples require product-level sample and price checks.

### Flights 22–24: advanced storage and market literacy

- Seek the same identifiable production across Kunming, Taiwan/Guangdong, and Malaysia only where storage provenance can be stated; otherwise omit the false match.
- Use traditional Hong Kong examples for storage literacy and compare a famous-area claim with credible broad-region material only after documenting what each claim actually says.

## Anchor selection rationale

The provisional anchor strategy follows the approved spiral curriculum: a compact set of repeatable, documented-or-clearly-qualified productions should recur across core flights. Mid-2000s Dayi 7542/8582 and an Xiaguan T8653 are search anchors because TeaDB identifies them as broadly available factory baselines; they are not anchors until a current offer confirms identity, storage, sample size, price, and stock. A storage pair is an anchor only when it is genuinely the same production and batch.

## Rejected candidates

- Do not enter a generic recipe label (for example, an unlabeled “7542”) without evidence that it is the stated factory production and batch.
- Do not use a city name as a substitute for storage provenance.
- Do not use cheap famous-village or highly specific old-arbor claims as terroir proof; retain them only as explicitly caveated market-literacy examples if a future flight needs one.
- Do not use a cake-only offer for an essential path unless no credible sample exists and the limitation is explicit.
- Do not turn TeaDB's historical price references, ratings, or vendor mentions into current prices, stock status, or documented provenance.

## Research caveats

- TeaDB provides practitioner interpretation and discovery; vendor product pages provide time-sensitive stock, size, currency, price, and stated storage facts.
- A vendor's stated origin, village, tree age, storage, or batch is a **vendor claim** unless the offer supplies stronger documentation. Sensory descriptions are **practitioner expectations**, not controlled results.
- Production year, recipe, producer, leaf material, compression, processing, storage environment, and sampling history can all confound a comparison.
- TeaDB sources span 2014–2026. Their vendor and price commentary can age quickly, so the catalog must carry its own checked date and no unverified purchasing fact is implied here.
