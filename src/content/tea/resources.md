#### Links (In No Particular Order)
<a href="https://www.teatechnique.org/">Tea Technique</a> – <i>An Introduction to the Art & Science of Chinese 
Tea Ceremony</i> by Jason M. Cohen is an in-depth e-book exploring Chinese tea culture from multiple angles. It takes 
you on a journey from the origins and history of tea all the way to the chemical makeup of various zisha clays. A
must-read for anyone who takes their tea hobby seriously. The full version of the book is available on a subscription 
basis, however, some chapters of it are in open access. They also have 
<a href="https://open.spotify.com/show/0045GU8ZzPjrKa7Yuvw5PK?si=5ca17cfd608b4859">a podcast on Spotify</a> and, I 
believe YoutTube (?). All free. And I am really hooked on it.

<a href="https://www.facebook.com/groups/teapot2"> Early Teapots II </a> – a Facebook group dedicated to discussions 
surrounding the book <i>Early Teapots Encyclopedia Vol. II</i>, moderated by its author, Chi Lin Lu. The group holds a 
tremendous amount of information on antique Yixing teapots (especially F1), much of which is difficult to find 
elsewhere—provided you’re willing to dig through hundreds of posts and thousands of comments. Most discussions are in 
Chinese, which adds an extra layer of challenge, but those undeterred will uncover a true well of knowledge.

<a href="https://www.teapotandtea.com/"> Teapot and Tea </a> – a blog of another tea enthusiast Youzi Lao where you can 
find a few good article about zisha and its art

<a href="https://teadb.org/"> TeaDB </a> – a long-running blog by two teaheads, James Schergen and Denny Chapin. It is 
full of tea explorations and discussions about vendors. I learned a lot from them about which teas are worth trying and 
why, and which vendors are the best in class for each type of tea. They also have
<a href="http://www.youtube.com/user/teadborg?feature=watch">a YouTube channel where they do a lot of tea tastings</a>.

<a href="https://www.donghetea.com/"> DongheTea< /a> – a major Chinese secondary-market price tracker and trading platform 
for Pu-erh, based in Guangzhou’s Fangcun tea market. The easiest way to think of it is as something between a Pu-erh 
price index, a broker, and a marketplace. It became especially influential for Dayi/Taetea (大益) products. When I want 
to buy a good Pu-erh cake, or estimate the premium a vendor is charging, I check it to get a price baseline.

<a href="https://globalteahut.org/"> Global Tea Hut </a> – a Taiwan-based tea education and community organization, 
magazine, and tea shop, with a strong focus on Cha Dao, Zen and Daoist philosophy, tea ceremony, traditional teaware, 
and self-cultivation through tea. Their mission explicitly combines tea, teaware, education, and community, with 
particular emphasis on the relationship between Zen, Daoism, and tea. I love it for two reasons. First, the old 
editions of their journal, which are available for free, are a mine of knowledge on all kinds of tea-related subjects, 
including teaware, history, teas, and tea regions. Second, it is a great place to discover modern tea artisans and their
work.
