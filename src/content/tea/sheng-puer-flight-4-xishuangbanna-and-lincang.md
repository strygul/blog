## Why This Set

The close commercial format makes the comparison practical and limits some obvious sources of noise. It also places a
Lincang example beside a Xishuangbanna example without a large prestige or price mismatch.

The pair is intentionally an orientation. If the teas differ clearly, the observation still belongs to these two
samples. If they overlap or the contrast does not repeat, that is equally valid information.

## Set Up the Flight

Use two identical neutral porcelain vessels, two matching cups, a scale, timer, one kettle, and one water source.

1. Weigh **5 g of each tea** into vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml**.
2. Equalize fragment size and leaf integrity as far as practical and note visible differences.
3. Assign neutral codes A and B. Keep the product pages, map, and expected regional descriptions out of view.
4. Preheat both vessels identically and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give each tea a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Use one fixed pouring sequence per
   session and mark mistimed infusions.

Randomize positions before the first round and again before the repeat. Complete the first notes and preference ranking
before consulting regional expectations.

## What to Observe

Record each code on the same dimensions:

- dry-leaf, warmed-leaf, liquor, and cup aroma;
- bitterness onset, intensity, location, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture and apparent body;
- finish and cooling behavior;
- perceived development and infusion-to-infusion change.

Avoid turning one striking note into a regional marker. The principal contrast should be a pattern across several
dimensions or infusions, not a single expected word.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the comparison on another day with new codes and positions. Keep the same water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml ratio,
temperature, rinse, infusion schedule, cooling time, and pouring sequence.

The principal contrast must be reasonably repeatable to count as useful. If it is unstable, contradictory, or unclear,
report the result as **inconclusive** rather than selecting the interpretation that best fits the labels.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **this is how the selected Xishuangbanna- and Lincang-labelled examples differed under one
session protocol**.

The flight cannot establish a complete regional typology, verify either origin, or isolate geography from harvest,
material, processing, compression, and storage.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

Keep seller, producer label, year, season, portion size, and price close while preserving one Xishuangbanna and one
Lincang label. Avoid replacing either tea with a prestige sample that creates a large price or harvest mismatch.

These are single-flight purchases, although their product-page claims remain useful in Lab B.
