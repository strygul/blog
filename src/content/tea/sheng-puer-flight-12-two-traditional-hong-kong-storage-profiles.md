## What This Flight Asks

How do two complete teas with explicit Yee On traditional Hong Kong warehouse descriptions differ under one blind,
repeatable protocol?

Both pages name traditional Hong Kong warehouse treatment and provide practical sample portions. Their productions and
handling histories differ, which makes the comparison one of whole stored expressions rather than a controlled storage
gradient.

## Why This Set

One seller provides explicit traditional Hong Kong warehouse narratives and small portions for both teas. That makes
the pages and cups useful after the storage-evidence lab has established what such documentation can and cannot support.

Producer, recipe, year, source material, initial condition, cellar duration, rotation, measured conditions, packaging,
custody, and any post-cellar airing differ or remain unknown. Each tea must be interpreted as a whole profile.

## Set Up the Flight

Use two identical neutral porcelain vessels, two matching cups, a precise scale, timer, one kettle, and the same water.

1. Give both samples the same rest after opening and weigh **5 g of each tea**.
2. Use vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml** and select comparable fragments.
3. Assign neutral codes A and B. Hide the production names, years, prices, and warehouse descriptions.
4. Preheat both vessels identically and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give each tea a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep rinse count, water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Use a fixed pouring sequence
   and mark mistimed infusions.

Do not add an extra rinse to one tea during the controlled phase. If an exploratory extra rinse seems useful, perform
it only after the comparison and label the resulting notes separately.

## What to Observe

Record each code on the same dimensions:

- dry-leaf, warmed-leaf, liquor, wet-leaf, and emptied-cup aroma;
- liquor color and clarity;
- bitterness onset, intensity, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture, body, and mouth coating;
- finish, cooling behavior, and perceived development;
- direction and pace of change through the infusion sequence.

Do not require either cup to display a presumed traditional-storage marker. A recurring observation is evidence about
these samples, not authentication of the warehouse narrative.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the complete flight on another day with new codes and cup positions. Hold water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml ratio,
temperature, rinse count, infusion schedule, resting time, cooling time, and pouring sequence constant.

The principal contrast should recur despite the new coding. If it disappears, reverses, or remains unclear, report the
flight as **inconclusive**. The limited 10 g sample makes careful execution especially important.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **this is how two complete, vendor-described traditional Hong Kong storage expressions
differed under this protocol**.

The flight cannot establish a single storage-intensity, cellar-duration, or airing effect; reconstruct measured
warehouse conditions or custody; generalize a Hong Kong profile; or authenticate either production through flavor.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

Require two pages that explicitly document traditional Hong Kong warehouse treatment and offer enough tea for two 5 g
sessions. Treat each tea as a complete profile unless production and handling chronology are genuinely matched. A city
label without a warehouse description is not an equivalent substitute.

Both pages and teas also support Lab A and are counted only once in the complete program basket.
