## What This Flight Asks

How do one 2025 Yiwu-labelled tea and one 2025 Bulang-labelled tea from the same seller differ under a shared protocol?

This pair should make a direct sensory comparison possible because seller, producer label, year, sample size, young-raw
format, and price tier are close. The Yiwu tea is a single-area-labelled sample, while the Bulang tea is described as a
blend, so region is not the only variable.

## Why This Set

A broad regional comparison becomes easier to interpret when obvious purchasing variables are close. Using one seller
and house label also reduces differences in naming convention and sample preparation. It does not control origin,
material, tree age, blend status, exact harvest timing, processing, compression, or storage.

The educational aim is therefore modest: create two reference points, observe whether their main differences repeat,
and resist converting those two points into a complete map of either region.

## Set Up the Flight

Use two identical neutral porcelain vessels, two matching cups, a scale, timer, one kettle, and one water source.

1. Weigh **5 g of each tea** into vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml**.
2. Choose similarly intact fragments. Record unavoidable differences in leaf breakage rather than altering the dose.
3. Assign neutral codes A and B, preferably through another person. Hide product names and regional labels.
4. Preheat both vessels equally and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give both teas a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Do not brew the tea expected to be
   stronger more gently.

Randomize cup position. Use a fixed pouring sequence within a session and reverse or newly randomize positions for the
repeat so placement does not become the identity cue.

## What to Observe

For each code, follow the same dimensions through the infusion sequence:

- aroma families in the leaf, liquor, and emptied cup;
- bitterness onset, location, shape, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture and apparent body;
- finish, salivation, and cooling behavior;
- the pace and direction of change across infusions.

Describe first; interpret later. Do not require either tea to fit a remembered regional profile.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the full comparison on a different day with new codes and positions. Hold the water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml
ratio, temperature, rinse, infusion schedule, cooling time, and pouring sequence constant.

Look for recurrence in the principal contrast rather than identical prose. If the contrast reverses, disappears, or is
too weak to describe confidently, record the outcome as **inconclusive**.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **this is how the selected, closely matched Yiwu- and Bulang-labelled teas differed under
this protocol**.

The flight cannot establish a sensory law for Yiwu or Bulang, authenticate origin or tree age, or isolate geography as
the cause. Single-area versus blend construction and other unmatched variables remain plausible explanations.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

Prefer one seller and producer label, the same harvest year and season, equal sample format, and a close price tier.
Preserve a broad Yiwu/Mengla versus Bulang/Menghai label and disclose blend status. If those controls cannot be kept,
postpone the flight rather than using a prestige mismatch.
