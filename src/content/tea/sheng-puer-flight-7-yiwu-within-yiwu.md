## What This Flight Asks

How do three selected 2025 Yiwu/Mengla-labelled subregional examples vary under the shared protocol?

This is an advanced orientation to within-region variation. It does not ask the taster to identify villages blindly,
nor does it assume that one sample represents every tea carrying the same place name.

## Why This Set

Using three samples reduces the temptation to treat a single contrast as a fixed regional axis. One tea may differ from
the other two, all three may separate, or substantial overlap may be the most important result.

The set is commercially coherent, but origin, tree age, leaf population, lot composition, processing, compression, and
storage remain unverified or incompletely matched.

## Set Up the Flight

Use three identical neutral porcelain vessels, three matching cups, a scale, timer, one kettle, and one water source.

1. Weigh **5 g of each tea** into vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml**.
2. Choose similarly situated, comparably intact fragments and note unavoidable differences.
3. Have another person assign neutral codes A, B, and C. Hide subregional names and prices.
4. Preheat all vessels identically and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give every tea a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Use a fixed pouring sequence within each
   session and mark obvious timing errors.

Randomize initial cup positions. Rotate positions between infusions only if the movement can be performed reliably; if
not, keep positions stable within the session and assign new positions for the repeat.

## What to Observe

For all three codes, record:

- leaf, liquor, and emptied-cup aroma;
- bitterness onset, location, intensity, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture, apparent body, and salivation;
- aftertaste and cooling behavior;
- extraction curve, late-infusion persistence, and degree of overlap within the set.

Do not look for predetermined village markers. Describe similarities as carefully as differences.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the complete comparison on a different day with new codes and cup positions. Hold water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml
ratio, temperature, rinse, infusion schedule, cooling time, and pouring sequence constant.

Look for repeatable relative patterns, including repeatable overlap. If the main ordering or contrast changes
substantially, report the flight as **inconclusive**.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **these are the observed differences and overlaps among the selected same-vendor/year
Yiwu/Mengla subregional labels**.

The flight cannot establish fixed subregional signatures, authenticate village origin or tree age, or isolate geography
from leaf population, lot, processing, compression, and storage.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

Use at least three Yiwu/Mengla-labelled samples from one seller and producer label with the same year, season, portion,
and close price. Prefer explicit lot and processing documentation. If only two valid samples remain, postpone this
three-point flight rather than presenting a binary comparison under the same question.
