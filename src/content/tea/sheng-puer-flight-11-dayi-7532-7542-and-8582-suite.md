## What This Flight Asks

How do the selected 2008 Dayi 7532, 7542, and 8582 samples differ under one blind, repeatable protocol?

Three cups make the relative variation among the selected recipe labels easier to see than a single 7542–8582 axis.
The 7542 batch ambiguity and unmatched storage wording remain central limits.

## Why This Set

Producer, seller, stated year, small-sample format, and broad storage city make the suite practical. Reusing two teas
from Flight 5 also lets the taster see whether adding a third cup changes the earlier interpretation.

Batch, material, blend construction, processing, compression, sampling history, storage, and custody remain
incompletely matched. Recipe-number leaf-grade digits are identifiers within a convention, not sensory or quality
scores.

## Set Up the Flight

Use three identical neutral porcelain vessels, three matching cups, a scale, timer, one kettle, and one water source.

1. Weigh **5 g of each tea** despite the 30 g versus 25 g retail sample sizes.
2. Use vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml** and select comparable fragments.
3. Have another person assign neutral codes A, B, and C. Hide recipe, batch, storage, and price information.
4. Preheat all vessels equally and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give every tea a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Use a fixed pouring sequence and mark
   obvious timing errors.

Randomize positions for each session. Do not try to identify the recipes from conventional descriptions while making
the blind notes.

## What to Observe

For every code, record:

- dry- and wet-leaf, liquor, and cup aroma;
- bitterness onset, intensity, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture, body, and liquor weight;
- aftertaste and cooling behavior;
- extraction pattern and persistence across infusions;
- similarities as well as differences within the three-tea set.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the complete suite on a different day with new codes and cup positions. Hold water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml ratio,
temperature, rinse, infusion schedule, cooling time, and pouring sequence constant.

Look for repeatable relative patterns, not a forced total ranking. If the main relationships disappear, reverse, or
remain unclear, report the suite as **inconclusive**.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **this is an orientation to the observed differences and overlaps among the selected 2008
Dayi recipe samples**.

The flight cannot establish a batch-controlled recipe effect, universal recipe signatures, a leaf-grade quality order,
or the 7542 batch. The result remains specific to these samples and their partly unmatched histories.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

Prefer all three Dayi recipes from one documented year and batch family, seller, sample format, and storage history. If
one batch is ambiguous, state that prominently. Do not mix years merely to complete the trio, and do not infer a batch
from the URL alone.

The 7542 and 8582 are reused from Flight 5 and counted only once in the complete basket.
