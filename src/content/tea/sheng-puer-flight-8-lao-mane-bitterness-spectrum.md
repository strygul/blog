## What This Flight Asks

How do the selected sweet-varietal- and bitter-varietal-labelled Lao Man’e teas differ in the onset, shape, duration, and
recovery of bitterness?

Bitterness is not recorded as a single intensity score. The flight separates when it begins, where and how it is felt,
how it changes in the mouth, how long it persists, and what follows it. Preference is recorded separately.

## Why This Set

The pair preserves the same stated year, named village, sample size, young-raw format, and explicit sweet/bitter
varietal contrast. It is the best available orientation found in the purchasing survey, but the changed producer and
unmatched material disclosures prevent a varietal-only conclusion.

The flight remains worthwhile because bitterness can be tasted and described directly. What must stay modest is the
explanation, not the observation.

## Set Up the Flight

Use two identical neutral porcelain vessels, two matching cups, a scale, timer, one kettle, and the same water.

1. Weigh **5 g of each tea** into vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml**.
2. Select comparably intact fragments and record differences in leaf condition.
3. Assign neutral codes A and B. Hide vendor, varietal, material, and price claims.
4. Preheat both vessels equally and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give each tea a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Do not soften the bitter-labelled tea by
   changing its ratio, temperature, or timing.

Randomize cup position and use a fixed pouring sequence. Taste at equal cooling intervals because temperature can
change both perceived intensity and texture.

## What to Observe

Give bitterness its own structured record for every infusion:

- onset: immediate, delayed, or building after swallowing;
- peak intensity and the infusion in which it occurs;
- location in the mouth and throat;
- texture: sharp, spreading, drying, dense, or another neutral description;
- duration and rate of recovery;
- returning sweetness after the bitter sensation;
- associated astringency, salivation, texture, and finish;
- change in all dimensions across the infusion sequence.

No outcome is prescribed. A “sweet-varietal” label does not require an absence of bitterness, and intensity alone does
not determine quality or preference.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the full comparison on another day with new codes and cup positions. Hold water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml ratio,
temperature, rinse, infusion schedule, cooling time, and pouring sequence constant.

The central pattern should recur across sessions. If onset, peak, or recovery reverses or becomes unclear, report the
flight as **inconclusive** rather than choosing the round that best matches the varietal labels.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **this is how bitterness onset, intensity, texture, duration, recovery, and returning
sweetness differed between the selected teas**.

The flight cannot establish varietal or tree-age authenticity, a village signature, or a single cause for bitterness.
It also cannot generalize one cross-vendor pair to all sweet- and bitter-varietal Lao Man’e teas.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

First seek purchasable same-producer, same-year Lao Man’e bitter- and sweet-varietal samples with matched format and
material disclosure. A cross-vendor fallback must retain the same year, village, portion size, explicit varietal labels,
and this lower inference ceiling. A generic sweet Yiwu tea is not a substitute.

Neither purchase is required elsewhere, although both pages can be classified in Lab B.
