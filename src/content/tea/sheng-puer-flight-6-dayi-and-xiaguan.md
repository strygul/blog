## What This Flight Asks

How do selected 2007 Dayi and Xiaguan factory examples differ under the same blind brewing protocol?

The flight can produce a direct comparison while teaching an important limit: producer changes alongside recipe,
material, processing, compression, and possibly storage. A sensory difference cannot be assigned to the factory name
alone.

## Why This Set

Seller, stated year, broad storage location, and 25 g format are matched. That makes the side-by-side practical, but the
pair still combines different recipes, factories, source materials, processing, compression, and incompletely known
custody.

Treat extraction pace as an observation, not a shortcut to a compression or factory label. The exercise asks how these
two samples differ, not which producer is better.

## Set Up the Flight

Use two identical neutral porcelain vessels, two matching cups, a scale, timer, one kettle, and the same water.

1. Weigh **5 g of each tea** into vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml**.
2. Separate comparable fragments as gently as possible. If one sample requires exceptional breaking, document it.
3. Assign neutral codes A and B and hide all producer, recipe, and price information.
4. Preheat the vessels identically and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give each tea a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Do not compensate for compression by
   changing only one tea's timing during the controlled phase.

Use a fixed pouring sequence and randomized cup positions. A later exploratory infusion may use different parameters,
but label it separately from the controlled comparison.

## What to Observe

Record each code across the following dimensions:

- dry- and wet-leaf, liquor, and cup aroma;
- extraction pace across the first three infusions;
- bitterness onset, intensity, and duration;
- astringency location and persistence;
- sweetness, returning sweetness, and finish;
- texture, body, and cooling behavior;
- persistence and change across later infusions.

Do not use any single dimension as a producer-authentication marker.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the flight on another day with new codes and positions. Keep water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml ratio, temperature,
rinse, infusion schedule, cooling time, leaf preparation, and pouring sequence as consistent as possible.

A useful contrast should recur in direction and timing. If it does not, report the comparison as **inconclusive** rather
than explaining the inconsistency with a factory narrative.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **this is how the selected same-year Dayi and Xiaguan examples differed under this
protocol**.

The flight cannot establish factory essence, producer authentication by taste, recipe or compression as the cause, or
an objective quality ranking. The samples are multivariable factory examples.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

Keep Dayi and Xiaguan examples in the same year, seller, portion size, and storage history as closely as possible.
Record exact recipe, batch, and compression only when the page documents them; never infer form from a URL.

The Dayi sample is reused from Flight 1 and is counted only once in the complete purchasing basket.
