## What This Flight Asks

How do one seller's 2025 spring and autumn Jiu Tai Po samples differ under the same blind protocol?

Seller, producer label, named origin, year, sample format, tree-age claim, and broad processing description are closely
matched. The paired pages make season a more focused question than a comparison between unrelated spring and autumn
teas.

Harvest lot, weather, exact picking date, leaf grade, processing, pressing, storage, and parent-cake size may differ.
Those variables prevent a season-only conclusion.

## Why This Set

Few retail pairs hold this many descriptors close while differing explicitly by spring and autumn. That makes the set
suitable for testing whether a stable contrast exists and whether “season-associated” is a useful cautious description.

The vendor's own comparative tasting notes should not set the score. The blind protocol comes before the page narrative
so the observation is not simply a repetition of expected seasonal differences.

## Set Up the Flight

Use two identical neutral porcelain vessels, two matching cups, a scale, timer, one kettle, and one water source.

1. Give both samples the same resting time after opening.
2. Weigh **5 g of each tea** into vessels with a practical brewing volume of **100 ml**.
3. Choose comparable fragments and assign neutral codes A and B. Hide the seasonal labels and prices.
4. Preheat both vessels identically and use water at **100 °C**.
5. Give each tea a five-second rinse, then brew for **10, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 45 seconds**.
6. Keep water volume, pour speed, cooling time, and drinking order consistent. Use one fixed pouring sequence and mark
   any timing error.

Randomize cup position for the first session and assign new positions for the repeat. Do not treat spring as the default
winner or autumn as a lower-quality control.

## What to Observe

Record each code across:

- dry-leaf, warmed-leaf, liquor, and cup aroma;
- bitterness onset, intensity, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture and apparent body;
- finish, cooling behavior, and salivation;
- extraction curve and persistence across later infusions.

Separate descriptive observations from preference. A preference may repeat even when the descriptive contrast does
not, and the two findings should not be conflated.

## Repeat Before Drawing a Conclusion

Repeat the full flight on another day with new codes and cup positions. Hold water, vessels, 5 g / 100 ml ratio,
temperature, rinse, infusion schedule, resting time, cooling time, and pouring sequence constant.

A season-associated interpretation requires the principal contrast to recur. If it disappears, reverses, or remains
unclear, report the result as **inconclusive**.

## What the Result Can Support

The allowed conclusion is: **a plausible season-associated contrast was—or was not—repeatably observed in this matched
commercial pair**.

The flight cannot establish season as the sole cause, a universal spring/autumn rule, verified origin or tree age, or
the superiority of either season.

## If One Sample Is Unavailable

Require the same seller, producer, named origin, year, processing style, sample format, and closely described material,
with the pages differing explicitly by spring versus autumn. If those controls fail, omit the flight rather than
substituting an unrelated seasonal pair.

Both product pages can also support the claims-classification work in Lab B.
