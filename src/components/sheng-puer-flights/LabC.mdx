<h2>Question</h2>

How does learning a tea's name and price change this participant's sensory score, preference, and estimated value?

<h2>Materials</h2>

- [2025 Yunnan Sourcing Bing Dao Lao Zhai](https://yunnansourcing.com/products/2025-yunnan-sourcing-bing-dao-lao-zhai-raw-pu-erh-tea-cake) — 10 g, USD 9.00 / €7.76. The sample offer is observed; origin, tree age, production size, and sensory claims are vendor claims.
- [2025 Yunnan Sourcing Mo Lie Shan](https://yunnansourcing.com/products/2025-yunnan-sourcing-mo-lie-shan-raw-pu-erh-tea-cake) — 25 g, USD 7.60 / €6.55. The offer is observed; nearby Mengku geography, origin, material, and sensory claims are vendor claims. This is a different 2025 production from Flight 9's 2024 Mo Lie.

<h2>Procedure</h2>

Have another person randomize the samples under neutral codes. Brew one blind round with the shared protocol, then record a sensory score, preference, and estimated value before revealing either identity. Reveal the names and prices, explain the unequal purchase sizes and per-gram source prices, then repeat the evaluation and record score changes. Repeat on another day if the first response is unstable.

<h2>Record</h2>

Record blind and informed sensory scores, preference order, estimated value, confidence, and the signed change for each measure. Keep sensory observations separate from value judgments.

<h2>Supported Conclusion</h2>

Only the participant's expectation response to this name-and-price disclosure is measured.

<h2>Cannot Establish</h2>

The lab cannot show that price, prestige, scarcity, authenticity, origin, or objective quality was tasted, nor generalize one participant's response to other people.
