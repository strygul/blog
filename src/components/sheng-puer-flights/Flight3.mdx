<h2>Why This Set</h2>

A broad regional comparison becomes easier to interpret when obvious purchasing variables are close. Using one seller
and house label also reduces differences in naming convention and sample preparation. It does not control origin,
material, tree age, blend status, exact harvest timing, processing, compression, or storage.

The educational aim is therefore modest: create two reference points, observe whether their main differences repeat,
and resist converting those two points into a complete map of either region.

<h2>Flight-Specific Controls</h2>

Hide the regional labels and expected profiles. Do not brew the tea expected to be stronger more gently, and assign new
cup positions for the repeat so placement does not become an identity cue.

<h2>What to Observe</h2>

For each code, follow the same dimensions through the infusion sequence:

- aroma families in the leaf, liquor, and emptied cup;
- bitterness onset, location, shape, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture and apparent body;
- finish, salivation, and cooling behavior;
- the pace and direction of change across infusions.

Describe first; interpret later. Do not require either tea to fit a remembered regional profile.

<h2>What We Can Learn From It</h2>

The most we can say is: **this is how these two Yiwu- and Bulang-labelled teas differed when we brewed them in the same
way**.

These two teas cannot tell us what all Yiwu or Bulang tea tastes like. They also cannot prove where the tea came from,
how old the trees were, or that geography caused the differences we noticed. One is sold as a single-area tea and the
other as a blend, and plenty of other things differ between them.

To go further, we would need to taste many more teas from both places—not just one of each—and compare teas that are
reasonably similar in age, material, processing, and storage. If the same differences kept appearing across different
teas, years, and drinkers, we could begin to see a broader pattern. This flight is simply our first step.

<h2>If One Sample Is Unavailable</h2>

Prefer one seller and producer label, the same harvest year and season, equal sample format, and a close price tier.
Preserve a broad Yiwu/Mengla versus Bulang/Menghai label and disclose blend status. If those controls cannot be kept,
postpone the flight rather than using a prestige mismatch.
