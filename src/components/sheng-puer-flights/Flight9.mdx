<h2>Why This Set</h2>

The commercial match is close enough to make a focused within-prefecture comparison practical. Retaining the page
inconsistency also makes the flight a useful lesson in not converting loose geographical proximity into provenance.

The sensory task is independent of that uncertainty: the cups can differ repeatably even when the labels do not support
a precise regional cause.

<h2>Flight-Specific Controls</h2>

Keep maps, village pages, prices, and expected profiles hidden. Review the maps and documentation only after the first
blind descriptions and preference ranking are committed.

<h2>What to Observe</h2>

Record for each code:

- leaf, liquor, and emptied-cup aroma;
- bitterness onset, intensity, and duration;
- sweetness and returning sweetness;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture, body, and salivation;
- finish and cooling behavior;
- extraction curve and change through later infusions.

Do not search for a predetermined Mengku- or Bangdong-side marker. The comparison is successful if it produces an
honest, repeatable description—even if the samples substantially overlap.

<h2>What We Can Learn From It</h2>

This tasting shows us how these Mengku- and Bangdong-side examples differed, while keeping the unclear Na Han location in mind.

Two teas cannot draw a sensory boundary across Lincang or settle the conflicting location information on the vendor page. We also cannot confirm origin or tree age from taste, and lot, processing, compression, and storage may explain some of what we noticed.

To learn more, we would need many well-documented examples from both sides, across producers and years, with storage matched as closely as possible. Checking the place labels carefully and repeating the tasting blind would help us see whether the same patterns return.
