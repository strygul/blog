<h2>Why This Set</h2>

Storage comparisons are often weakened by using different productions. Here, the seller presents the samples as two
portions of the same named year, producer, and production. That makes a storage-associated interpretation more
plausible if a contrast repeats, while still leaving the documentation and handling gaps visible.

The goal is not to identify a “Taiwan taste” or a “USA taste.” It is to determine whether these two particular portions
can be distinguished in a stable way and to describe the difference without assigning more certainty than the match
supports.

<h2>Flight-Specific Controls</h2>

Do not air, condition, or rest only one portion before comparing them. Do not add an extra rinse to the portion expected
to have received more humid storage during the controlled phase.

<h2>What to Observe</h2>

Record the following without guessing the storage label:

- dry-leaf, warmed-leaf, liquor, and wet-leaf aroma;
- liquor color and clarity;
- bitterness onset, intensity, and duration;
- astringency location and persistence;
- texture and apparent body;
- sweetness, returning sweetness, and finish;
- perceived development and change across infusions;
- whether the main contrast remains stable as the cups cool.

<h2>What the Result Can Support</h2>

The allowed conclusion is: **a plausible storage-associated difference was—or was not—repeatably observed within this
seller's matched commercial set**.

The flight cannot establish measured humidity or temperature effects, exact custody, a universal Taiwan or USA storage
profile, or storage as the only cause. Flavor also cannot authenticate the production or either storage label.

<h2>If the Set Is Unavailable</h2>

Require two portions of the same documented production with distinct seller-described storage histories. Matching the
year alone is not enough. If production identity cannot be held, use the pages as material for the storage-evidence lab
rather than presenting the teas as a replacement for this flight.
